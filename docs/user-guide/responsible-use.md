# Responsible use

Histmersion helps you collect, read and connect sources. It does not decide what is true, and it
does not make anything legal that is not. This page lists the rules that come with it. Read it
before you start, and again before you share anything you made with it.

## 1. Get sources legally, and respect the sites you use

- **Only legal copies.** When Histmersion downloads a paper, it asks only open-access services
  (Unpaywall, CORE, OpenAlex, Europe PMC and PubMed Central, arXiv, repository pages, public
  archive.org items) — see [Open-access papers](how-to/open-access-papers.md). It refuses
  archive.org items that are only lent out. No source that distributes copyrighted works without
  permission ships with Histmersion.
- **Pay for what is paid.** When there is no legal free copy, the work goes on your *acquisition
  list*: buy it, borrow it from a library, use your institution's access, or ask the author. Then
  bring your own file in ([Books and Calibre](how-to/books-and-calibre.md)). Paid services and APIs
  are used only with your own credentials ([Credentials and secrets](how-to/credentials.md)).
- **Follow each site's terms of use and rate limits.** Histmersion asks every outside service
  politely: one request at a time per host, waiting when a server asks it to wait, never working
  around a bot check. Do not change its settings to go faster than a service allows, and do not
  point it at a service whose terms forbid automated access.
- **Honour picture licences.** Pictures are stored only when their licence allows reuse (public
  domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA by default), and each keeps its credit line. If you reuse a picture,
  give that credit and follow its licence — see [Pictures](exploring/media.md).
- **Datasets have licences too.** Place, period and event data come with their own terms (some are
  non-commercial, some ask to be cited). [Open data](how-to/open-data.md) lists them.

## 2. AI models make mistakes

Histmersion can use AI language models (LLMs) to write summaries, extract findings, draft articles
and review them. This is optional ([Do I need AI?](getting-started/ai-optional.md)). When you use it:

- **Summaries, findings and articles can be wrong.** A model can misread a page, invent a detail,
  merge two authors' views, give a claim to the wrong source, or drop the nuance that mattered.
- **The review panel reduces errors; it does not remove them.** Three automated reviewers check
  every article, and checks written in code verify quotes and citations where they can. They still
  miss things. A "passed" review is not a guarantee.
- **Your assistant can change data.** An AI assistant connected through MCP or the command line can
  write to your collection. Review what it writes, and use the read-only tools (search, `query`)
  when you only want to explore. See [Using your AI assistant](ai-assistant.md).

## 3. Articles are where your research starts, not where it ends

- **Check every claim yourself.** Each sentence of an article names the source it rests on. Open
  that source, find the passage, and read it in context before you rely on the claim.
- **Never trust an article blindly** — not one you compiled with an agent, not one someone sent you.
- **Do not use Histmersion to generate scientific papers for publication.** It is a tool for
  finding and reading sources, not for producing scholarship on your behalf.

## 4. If you write your own text

You may use article drafts and summaries as a help while you write. Then:

- **cite properly every source you use**, in the citation style your field expects;
- **cite only sources you have read and checked yourself** — never a source you know only from a
  summary or an article;
- say so where your institution or publisher asks you to disclose the use of AI tools.

## 5. Absence is not evidence

Your collection holds only what you added. The place, period and event datasets cover some regions
and centuries far better than others, and every one of them is incomplete.

- **No source on a topic** means you have not collected one, not that none exists.
- **No event on the timeline** means no catalogue you imported lists one, not that nothing happened.
- **No place on the map** means the gazetteer lacks it, not that it did not exist.
- **No disagreement recorded** between two sources means nobody recorded one yet, not that the
  sources agree.

Treat every gap as a question to look into, never as an answer.
